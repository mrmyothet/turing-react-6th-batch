Debate Script: 
________________________________________
Moderator's Opening
Moderator: I greet everyone and am pleased to introduce today's debate on the important question: "Can sustainable tourism be achieved without detrimental impacts on the environment?" We have two teams prepared to delve into this issue. Team A will present arguments in favor of the possibility, while Team B will counter with reasons against it. Each team consists of three members. Let us commence with the opening statements from each side.
________________________________________
Round 1: Opening Statements
Speaker 1A (Team A - In Favor): Speaker 1A: Thank you. Respected members, sustainable tourism means traveling in ways that don’t hurt the environment and benefit local communities. We believe it is possible to achieve this by using green energy, protecting wildlife, and involving local people in tourism decisions. With proper planning and responsible behavior, tourists can enjoy nature without damaging it. Exemplary instances such as eco-friendly hotels and tours that respect nature can help both the environment and the local economy. There are 7 green projects The Thai government’s own initiatives. 
Speaker 1B (Team B - Against): Speaker 1B: Esteemed members, while sustainable tourism sounds great though, it’s not that simple. Even when we try to be eco-friendly, tourism can still harm the environment. More people travel resulting in increased pollution, wastage, and harm to natural areas. Plus, sometimes what’s called “sustainable tourism” is just a way to attract more tourists, which can lead to more harm than good. It’s very hard to have tourism without some negative impact on the environment like a mass tourism in Phuket and Pattaya. 
Speaker 2A (Team A - In Favor): Speaker 2A: I understand the concerns, but we must consider the progress that has been made. Today, we have eco-certifications that ensure hotels and tour companies meet strict environmental standards. There are also many examples of successful sustainable tourism, like nature reserves that use tourism money to fund conservation efforts. By educating tourists and advocating for responsible travel, we can minimize harm and even improve the environment. Here are some of our favorites for the environmental and wildlife protections: Trash Hero, New Heaven Reef Conservation and Soi Dog Foundation.
Speaker 2B (Team B - Against): Speaker 2B: Respectable attendees, there are indeed good examples, they are the exceptions rather than the rule. Tourism often leads to overcrowding and places strain on local resources and ecosystems. Additionally, the infrastructure supporting tourism, such as roads and hotels, significantly contributes to environmental degradation. These challenges underscore the complexities associated with achieving genuine sustainability in practice.
Speaker 3A (Team A - In Favor): Speaker 3A: One of the keys to truly sustainable tourism is involving local communities. When local people are in charge, they can make sure tourism benefits them and their environment. For example, in some areas, local guides lead eco-tours that educate visitors while protecting wildlife. These community-led initiatives show that it’s possible to balance tourism and environmental protection. When tourism is done right, it can provide jobs and funds for conservation, making it a win-win situation. For community-based tourism, Mae Kampong village is famous as a model of sustainability. 
Speaker 3B (Team B - Against): Speaker 3B: While community involvement is important, it doesn’t solve all the problems. Even small-scale tourism can have big impacts, particularly in fragile ecosystems. The escalation in waste, noise, and human activity can disrupt wildlife and degrade natural habitats. And as more tourists come, the pressure to expand tourism often leads to more environmental harm. Furthermore, the pursuit of economic growth through tourism often exacerbates environmental harm.
________________________________________






Thank you, Group A. Now let’s hear from Group B, who argues that sustainable tourism cannot be fully achieved without harming the environment.
Round 2: Rebuttals
Speaker 1A (Team A - In Favor): Speaker 1A: I understand my colleagues’ perspectives, but I strongly believe that we’re underestimating the potential of innovation. New technologies such as electric vehicles and renewable energy, can reduce the environmental impact of tourism. Moreover, strict regulations and responsible tourism practices can help control the negative effects. We must shift our focus to solutions and improvements rather than giving up on the idea of sustainable tourism altogether.
Speaker 1B (Team B - Against): Speaker 1B: Innovation and regulations are important, but they may not be sufficient. The reality is that tourism frequently expands more rapidly than our ability to manage its impact. Even with new technologies, the scale of modern tourism results in an unavoidable environmental impact. We should be cautious about assuming that technology alone can solve the complex problems associated with tourism and environmental damage.
Speaker 2A (Team A - In Favor): Speaker 2A: Even though tourism has expanded rapidly, it doesn’t mean we can’t work towards making it more sustainable. By shifting our attention to low-impact activities such as hiking, wildlife watching, and cultural tours, we can reduce the environmental strain. These activities not only reduce damage but also create awareness about the significance of protecting nature. Sustainable tourism revolves around making wiser decisions and promoting activities that have less impact on the environment.
Speaker 2B (Team B - Against): Speaker 2B: Even low-impact activities can have a significant effect when millions of people are involved. The large number of tourists visiting popular destinations often leads to the overuse of natural resources and the degradation of the environment. For example, even cautious hikers can contribute to soil erosion and disturb wildlife. In popular tourist spots, the pressure to accommodate more visitors can lead to harmful development, such as the construction of hotels in environmentally sensitive areas. It’s difficult to completely avoid these issues issue.

Round 3: Closing Arguments
Speaker 3A (Team A - In Favor): Speaker 3A: In conclusion, I believe that sustainable tourism is not only possible but essential. With careful planning, responsible practices, and community engagement, we can protect the environment while appreciating its beauty. It’s about making tourism work for nature, not against it. By continuing to innovate and educate, we can ensure that tourism yields positive outcomes for both people and the planet.
Speaker 3B (Team B - Against): Speaker 3B: To wrap up, while the idea of sustainable tourism is appealing, the reality is much more complex. The nature of tourism inherently imposes pressure on the environment, and even with the best intentions, there can be unintended consequences. We must be realistic about the challenges and recognize that achieving truly sustainable tourism without any environmental harm is extremely difficult, if not impossible. It’s essential to consider these realities as we think about the future of tourism.
